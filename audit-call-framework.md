# TR3 MEDIA SOLUTIONS — PAID AUDIT CALL FRAMEWORK

---

## OVERVIEW

**Format:** 45–60 minute paid strategy audit call
**Goal:** Diagnose where marketing is leaking money, attention, or conversions — then position a clear path to fix it
**Conversion Target:** 5 paid audits/month, convert 2 into strategy or execution engagements
**Tone:** Consultative, direct, human. Not salesy. Not corporate.

---

## CALL STRUCTURE (7 Sections)

| Section | Time | Purpose |
|---------|------|---------|
| 1. Open & Set the Frame | 3–5 min | Build trust, set expectations |
| 2. Business Snapshot | 8–10 min | Understand the business and goals |
| 3. Marketing Inventory | 10–12 min | Map what they're doing now |
| 4. Leak Diagnosis | 10–12 min | Find where money/attention/conversions are lost |
| 5. Reflect Back the Problem | 5–7 min | Mirror the issue clearly so they feel seen |
| 6. Recommendations & Quick Wins | 5–7 min | Give real value, show expertise |
| 7. Bridge to Engagement | 5–7 min | Transition to paid work naturally |

---

## SECTION 1: OPEN & SET THE FRAME (3–5 min)

**What to say:**

> "Thanks for making the time for this. Here's how I like to run these — I'm going to ask you some direct questions about your business and marketing. I'll take notes the whole time. By the end, I'll tell you exactly where I see the biggest gaps and what I'd prioritize fixing first. You'll walk away with clarity either way. Sound good?"

**Why this matters:** You're establishing authority and generosity from the first minute. You're also giving yourself permission to lead the conversation — which keeps you from overtaking.

**Key questions:**

1. "Before we dive in — what made you say yes to this call? What's the thing that's been nagging you?"
2. "What would make this hour worth every penny for you?"
3. "Is there anything specific you're hoping I'll look at today?"

**Rule for yourself:** Listen. Don't solve yet. Write down their exact words — you'll use them later.

---

## SECTION 2: BUSINESS SNAPSHOT (8–10 min)

**Goal:** Understand the business model, revenue drivers, and where they are vs. where they want to be.

**Key questions:**

1. "Walk me through how your business makes money. What are your main offers, and which ones drive the most revenue?"
2. "Who is your best customer — the one you'd clone ten times if you could? What makes them great?"
3. "What does your sales process look like right now? How do most customers find you and buy?"
4. "What's your revenue goal for the next 6–12 months, and what's the gap between where you are and where you want to be?"
5. "If you could only fix one thing in your business this quarter, what would it be?"

**Listen for:** Disconnects between what they sell and who they're marketing to. Vague answers about their customer. No clear sales process. Revenue goals with no plan attached.

---

## SECTION 3: MARKETING INVENTORY (10–12 min)

**Goal:** Map everything they're currently doing — paid, organic, content, email, partnerships, events. Find what exists and what's actually working.

**Key questions:**

1. "Walk me through everything you're spending money or time on for marketing right now — ads, social, email, events, content, anything."
2. "Which of those things is actually bringing in customers? How do you know?"
3. "What are you spending monthly on marketing — and do you know your cost to acquire a customer?"
4. "What's the last marketing thing you tried that didn't work? What happened?"
5. "Is there anything you've been meaning to start or fix but haven't gotten to?"

**Listen for:** Spending without tracking. Activity without strategy. Channels they can't connect to revenue. Things they're doing out of habit instead of results.

---

## SECTION 4: LEAK DIAGNOSIS (10–12 min)

**Goal:** This is where you earn the fee. Identify specific places their marketing is leaking money, attention, or conversions.

### THE LEAK DIAGNOSIS FRAMEWORK

Use these five categories to evaluate. Every marketing problem falls into at least one:

| Leak Type | What It Means | Signs |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| **Audience Leak** | Talking to the wrong people or too many people | Vague ICP, "everyone" is their customer, low engagement |
| **Message Leak** | The right people see it but don't care | Weak copy, features over outcomes, no clear value prop |
| **Channel Leak** | Spending time/money in the wrong places | Active on platforms their buyers don't use, no channel-revenue connection |
| **Conversion Leak** | Traffic or attention that doesn't turn into money | No CTA, broken funnel, no follow-up, slow response time |
| **Retention Leak** | Customers leave or never come back | No onboarding, no nurture, no referral system |

**Key questions:**

1. "When someone lands on your website or sees your social — is it immediately clear what you do, who it's for, and why they should care?"
2. "What happens after someone shows interest? Walk me through what happens between 'they're curious' and 'they pay you.'"
3. "Are you tracking where your leads actually come from, or is it mostly a guess?"
4. "How many leads or inquiries do you get per month that don't convert? Do you know why?"
5. "When was the last time you looked at your marketing and asked 'is this actually making me money?'"

**What to do:** As they answer, mentally (and on your template) check which leak types apply. Most businesses have 2–3 active leaks.

---

## SECTION 5: REFLECT BACK THE PROBLEM (5–7 min)

**Goal:** This is the most powerful part. Mirror what you've heard so they feel truly understood. Use their own words.

### THE REFLECTION SCRIPT

> "Okay, here's what I'm hearing. Tell me if I'm off. [Pause]
>
> You have a solid [business/offer/service] — that's not the problem. The problem is [specific leak].
>
> Right now, you're [what they're doing] but it's not [connecting to revenue / reaching the right people / converting / etc.] because [root cause].
>
> You said earlier that [use their exact words]. That tells me the real issue is [your diagnosis].
>
> The result is you're [spending money without knowing what's working / attracting the wrong leads / leaving revenue on the table / etc.].
>
> Does that feel accurate?"

**Example:**

> "You have a great service and your clients love you — that's clear. The problem isn't your work, it's that your marketing doesn't reflect the value of what you do. You're posting on Instagram three times a week, but there's no connection between that activity and someone actually booking a call. You mentioned you get DMs but don't have a system to move people from 'interested' to 'paying.' That's a conversion leak — attention is coming in, but money isn't coming out. Does that track?"

**Rule for yourself:** Say it. Then stop talking. Let them respond. Their response will tell you if you nailed it.

---

## SECTION 6: RECOMMENDATIONS & QUICK WINS (5–7 min)

**Goal:** Give 2–3 specific, actionable things they could do right now. This proves your value and builds trust.

**Framework for recommendations:**

1. **The Quick Win** — One thing they can fix this week that will make an immediate difference
2. **The Priority Fix** — The one leak that, if plugged, would have the biggest impact on revenue
3. **The System They're Missing** — The thing they need to build or implement that doesn't exist yet

> "Here's what I'd focus on if I were in your shoes. First, [quick win]. That's something you could do this week. Second, the bigger issue is [priority fix] — that's where the most money is leaking. Third, you're missing [system] entirely, and until you have that, you're going to keep hitting the same wall."

**Rule:** Be generous with insight. Be specific. Don't hold back to "save it for the engagement." The more value you give here, the more they'll want to work with you.

---

## SECTION 7: BRIDGE TO ENGAGEMENT (5–7 min)

**Goal:** Transition naturally from the audit into a conversation about working together. Not a pitch. A door.

### THE BRIDGE SCRIPT

> "So that's what I see. You've got a clear picture now of where things are breaking down, and you've got a couple things you can start on right away.
>
> Now — some people take this and run with it on their own, and that's great. But if you want help actually building this out — the strategy, the messaging, the system — that's exactly what I do.
>
> What I'd recommend for you is [specific scope: a 90-day strategy sprint / a messaging and funnel buildout / a full marketing reset]. We'd [brief description of what working together looks like].
>
> I can put together a proposal with scope, timeline, and investment. Want me to send that over?"

**If they say yes:**
> "Great. I'll have that to you by [specific day]. Take a couple days to sit with it, and we'll set up a short call to walk through it together if you have questions."

**If they hesitate:**
> "No pressure at all. You've got real clarity from today, and that's the point. If anything shifts or you want to revisit, I'm easy to reach. I'll still send over a recap of what we covered so you have it in writing."

**If they ask the price:**
> "It depends on the scope, but my strategy engagements typically start at [your range]. I'll put together something specific based on what we talked about today."

## SELF-COACHING REMINDERS

Tape these to your monitor during calls:

- **Ask, then shut up.** The prospect should talk 60–70% of the time.
- **Don't solve in real time.** Diagnose first. Recommendations come in Section 6.
- **Use their words.** When you reflect the problem back, quote them. It builds instant trust.
- **You're the doctor, not the salesperson.** Doctors diagnose, then recommend. That's your job.
- **Silence is your friend.** When you ask a big question, let it sit. Don't fill the gap.
- **One sentence, then check in.** If you catch yourself monologuing, stop and ask "Does that land?"
